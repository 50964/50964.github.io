Names are not just labels. They carry story, identity, and relationship. In Scripture, God’s names are how He makes Himself known.

Key idea

We identify people by their NAMES and STORIES.

The same is true of God. To know Him, we listen to the name He reveals and the story He tells.

Leader tip: Let people share their name meanings briefly — it opens the room before diving into Hebrew names.

Title vs Name

Hebrew foundations

ELOHIM
& YHWH

ELOHIM Means “God” in Hebrew — a title (deity), not a personal name. Power, majesty, Creator-God language.
YHWH / YAHWEH The personal covenant name. In English Bibles usually written LORD (small caps). The name by which He is to be known.
EHYEH / EHWEH “I will be / I am” — what God says of Himself at the bush (Exodus 3:14).

God says: EHWEH = I will be / AM
We say: YAHWEH = He is what He is

Curiosity is not the opposite of faith — often it’s the doorway.

Core Scripture

Turn to

EXODUS
2:23–25

“Now it happened in the process of time that the king of Egypt died. Then the children of Israel groaned because of the bondage, and they cried out; and their cry came up to God because of the bondage. So God heard their groaning, and God remembered His covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob. And God looked upon the children of Israel, and God acknowledged them.”

Exodus 2:23–25

Four verbs of God

  • Heard — their cry is not ignored
  • Remembered — covenant with Abraham, Isaac, Jacob
  • Looked — He sees His people
  • Acknowledged — He takes notice; He is involved

Before the burning bush, we already meet a God who is not distant. Elohim is attentive to pain and faithful to promise.

Read aloud: One voice for Israel’s cry, another for God’s four actions.

Exodus 3

Moses at the burning bush

I AM WHO
I AM

What’s Elohim going to do? He moves toward Moses — holy ground, personal call, mission to free His people.

“I am the God of your father—the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.”

Exodus 3:6

“I have surely seen the oppression… and have heard their cry… for I know their sorrows.”

Exodus 3:7

“I will be with you.” … “I am who I am… I am has sent me to you.”

Exodus 3:12, 14

Notice

  • Moses asks “Who am I?” — God answers with presence: “I will be with you.”
  • Moses asks for God’s name — God reveals I AM and YHWH, the God of the fathers.
  • Seen · heard · know — God is not abstract; He enters the story of the oppressed.

The name of God is not a puzzle to solve — it’s a Person to trust.

One LORD

From the bush to the Shema

THE NAME
TO KNOW

“I am the LORD (YHWH) your God (Elohim), who brought you out of Egypt… You shall have no other gods (elohim) before me.”

Deuteronomy 5:6–7

“Hear, O Israel: The LORD (YHWH) our God (Elohim), the LORD is one. Love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your strength.”

Deuteronomy 6:4–5 · The Shema

“…yet for us there is but one God, the Father… and there is but one Lord, Jesus Christ…”

1 Corinthians 8:5–6

The God who names Himself to Moses is the one God Israel is called to love fully — and the New Testament places Jesus within that same confession of one God and one Lord.

Gospel thread: The I AM who saves from Egypt is the Lord who saves through Jesus.

Small Group

Discussion

QUESTIONS
FOR THE ROOM

Honest conversation over perfect answers. 45–60 minutes.

Warm-up (5–8 min)

Q1 · Names

What does your name mean (or what story sits behind it)? How does it feel when someone uses your name well?

Q2 · Prayer

When you pray, who do you picture yourself speaking to? Has that picture changed over time?

Into the text (15–20 min)

Q3 · Title vs name

Why does it matter that Elohim is a title and YHWH is a personal name? How could that change the way someone approaches God?

Q4 · Exodus 2:23–25

Which of the four verbs — heard, remembered, looked, acknowledged — do you most need to believe about God right now? Why?

Q5 · Burning bush

Moses asks “Who am I?” God answers with “I will be with you.” When have you needed presence more than a full explanation?

Q6 · I AM

What does “I am who I am / I will be” say about God’s reliability in a world of shifting identities and broken promises?

Leader: If Q2 feels abstract, rephrase: “Who do you talk to when life hurts?”

Heart · Hands · Prayer

Application

LIVING THIS
WEEK

Heart questions (10–12 min)

Q7 · Distance

Where do you treat God more like a vague “force” (title only) than a personal YHWH who knows your name and sorrows?

Q8 · Cry

Is there a place of bondage or pain you’ve stopped crying out about? What would it look like to bring that cry to the God who hears?

Q9 · Mission fear

Moses felt inadequate. Where is God inviting you to step forward — and how does “I will be with you” speak into that?

Q10 · Shema

What would it look like this week to love the LORD with more of your heart, soul, or strength — in one concrete area?

Practice

  • Daily: Pray the Shema (Deut 6:4–5) slowly once a day.
  • Once: Write down a personal “name” for how you’ve known God (e.g. Provider, Friend) — then thank Him using YHWH’s revealed name: “LORD, you are…”
  • Once: Tell someone part of your story of how God heard or saw you.
  • Optional: Read Exodus 3 slowly and underline every time God says “I”.

Prayer prompts

  1. Thank God that He has a name and a story of rescue.
  2. Ask Him to be near in a place of sorrow or oppression.
  3. Pray for courage where you feel like Moses: “Who am I?”
  4. Worship Jesus as the one Lord through whom we live (1 Cor 8:6).

Watch-outs

  • Keep Hebrew simple — don’t get lost in pronunciation debates.
  • Don’t shame people who only know God as “God” — invite them deeper.
  • Connect clearly to Jesus for Christians who may not know OT names.

Key takeaway

We know people by names and stories. YHWH has given us both — so we don’t pray into the air, but to the God who is, who hears, and who is with us.
