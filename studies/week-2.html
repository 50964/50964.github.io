Last week: Names & stories

We identify people by their names and their stories. In Scripture, God’s names reveal His character — not just labels, but invitations to know Him.

  • Elohim — translates as God (title · deity)
  • YHWH — translates as LORD (personal · covenant name)

Elohim tells us who He is in power and majesty. YHWH tells us He draws near — a personal God who makes Himself known.

This week: Merciful & gracious

After the golden calf, Moses asks to see God’s glory. God doesn’t only show power — He proclaims His name and reveals His heart. This is one of the most important self-descriptions of God in the whole Bible.

Leader tip: Open with 2–3 minutes of silence or a short worship song, then read the passage slowly twice.

Core Scripture

Turn to

EXODUS
34:6–7

“And the Lord passed before him and proclaimed, ‘The LORD, the LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abounding in goodness and truth, keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin, by no means clearing the guilty, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children and the children’s children to the third and the fourth generation.’”

Exodus 34:6–7 (NKJV)

Notice

  • God speaks His own name — twice: YHWH, YHWH.
  • The list begins with mercy and grace, not anger.
  • Love does not cancel justice: He is forgiving and still does not clear the guilty.
  • Mercy is described as vast (“thousands”); judgment is real but measured.

This is the God who is both safe to run to and too holy to treat lightly.

Read aloud: One person reads v.6, another v.7, then the group sits with it for 30 seconds.

Teaching Frame

Holding both

LOVE &
JUSTICE

Exodus 34 doesn’t force us to choose between a soft God and a severe God. YHWH is merciful and just — perfectly both.

LOVE

  • 01 Merciful & gracious — He bends toward the broken
  • 02 Long-suffering — slow to anger, patient with us
  • 03 Goodness & truth — faithful, reliable, kind
  • 04 Keeping mercy — covenant love that lasts
  • 05 Forgiving sin — iniquity, transgression, and sin covered
  • 06 1,000 generations · forever — mercy’s reach is vast

JUSTICE

  • ! By no means clearing the guilty
  • → If we refuse mercy and harden ourselves, we stand accountable before Him
  • → “Don’t accept then: I will find you guilty.”
  • → God’s justice is personal and real — not random rage

The cross is where this tension resolves: justice satisfied, mercy poured out.

Curiosity is not the opposite of faith — often it’s the doorway.

Dig Deeper

Unpacking the attributes

WHAT GOD
REVEALS

1. Merciful & gracious

Mercy withholds what we deserve; grace gives what we don’t. God is not reluctant to forgive — it is who He is.

2. Long-suffering

Slow to anger. He gives space to repent. His patience is not permission to keep sinning; it is kindness leading us home (Romans 2:4).

3. Abounding in goodness & truth

His love is not soft-focus sentiment. It is full of hesed (loyal love) and emet (truth/faithfulness). He can be trusted.

4. Keeping mercy for thousands

Mercy multiplies across generations. The blessing of walking with God outlives our short lives.

5. Forgiving iniquity, transgression, and sin

Three words for the full mess of human failure — and God names Himself as the Forgiver of all of it.

6. Not clearing the guilty

Forgiveness is not indifference. Sin is serious. God will not pretend evil is fine. That is good news for victims and a warning for the unrepentant.

Gospel hinge: At the cross, the guilty can be justified — because Jesus bore guilt in our place.

Hard Text · Soft Heart

What about the children?

GENERATIONS
& GUILT

Exodus 34 mentions consequences to the third and fourth generation. Scripture also insists: each person is responsible for their own sin.

“Fathers shall not be put to death for their children, nor shall children be put to death for their fathers; a person shall be put to death for his own sin.”

Deuteronomy 24:16

“The soul who sins shall die. The son shall not bear the guilt of the father, nor the father bear the guilt of the son. The righteousness of the righteous shall be upon himself, and the wickedness of the wicked shall be upon himself.”

Ezekiel 18:20

How do we hold this together?

  • Patterns travel — family culture, trauma, and example shape the next generation.
  • Guilt is personal — God does not punish children as if they committed the parent’s sin.
  • Mercy can break the cycle — “thousands of generations” outruns “three or four.”
  • In Christ — we are not trapped by the story we inherited; we can receive a new one.

Your family story is not the final word. YHWH’s mercy can be.

Small Group

Discussion

QUESTIONS
FOR THE ROOM

Aim for honest conversation, not perfect answers. Leave space. Follow the Spirit. 45–60 minutes total.

Warm-up (5–8 min)

Q1 · Icebreaker

When you were younger, how did you picture God — more like a strict judge, a soft grandparent, distant boss, or something else? Has that picture changed?

Q2 · Names

Why might it matter that God reveals a personal name (YHWH / LORD) and not only a title (God / Elohim)?

Into the text (15–20 min)

Q3 · Read Exodus 34:6–7

Which word or phrase about God lands most strongly for you today — and why?

Q4 · Order matters

God lists mercy and grace before justice. What does that order teach us about His heart?

Q5 · Both / and

Where do you tend to lean — emphasising God’s love and soft-pedalling justice, or emphasising justice and struggling to receive mercy? What has shaped that lean?

Q6 · Generations

How do Deuteronomy 24:16 and Ezekiel 18:20 help us read the “third and fourth generation” language without despair — and without ignoring family patterns?

Leader: If the room goes quiet on Q5, share first to model honesty.

Heart · Hands · Prayer

Application

LIVING THIS
WEEK

Heart questions (10–12 min)

Q7 · Receiving mercy

Is there a place in your life where you struggle to believe God is merciful and gracious toward you? What would change if you took Him at His word?

Q8 · Refusing cheap grace

Is there an area where you have treated God’s patience as permission? What would repentance look like this week — specifically?

Q9 · Extending what we receive

Who in your world needs to meet the merciful side of God’s character through you this week — without you pretending sin doesn’t matter?

Q10 · Family stories

Where have you seen either destructive patterns or redeeming mercy travel through a family line? How does the gospel speak into that?

Practice (this week)

  • Daily: Pray Exodus 34:6 out loud each morning — “YHWH, merciful and gracious…”
  • Once: Confess one specific sin to God (and a trusted person if needed), and receive forgiveness.
  • Once: Offer a concrete act of mercy to someone who cannot repay you.
  • Optional: Journal: “Where have I seen God’s long-suffering in my story?”

Prayer prompts (close)

  1. Thank God for a specific mercy you have received.
  2. Ask for a soft heart where you have been hard toward Him or others.
  3. Pray for someone stuck in guilt — that they would meet YHWH’s forgiveness.
  4. Pray for justice and healing where sin has harmed people in your community.

Watch-outs

  • Don’t let the group turn “justice” into shame with no gospel hope.
  • Don’t let “mercy” become “sin doesn’t matter.”
  • On family patterns: be trauma-aware; no one must share private history.
  • Point to Jesus as the place love and justice meet (Romans 3:23–26).

Key takeaway line

YHWH is not half-love and half-justice. He is fully merciful and fully just — and at the cross, both are true for you.
