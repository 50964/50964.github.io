How to Use This Guide (Sticky Church Style)

This mini study guide is designed for C316's Thursday sermon-based small groups. Each week's questions follow Larry Osborne's proven "Sticky Church" format so the Sunday message actually sticks through discussion, community, and personal application.

The series "Jesus Never Meant That" exposes how Scripture is often twisted or misunderstood to support non-biblical living, false expectations, or a "gospel" that is not the gospel — and the faith that gets wrecked as a result.

The 3-Part Structure (use every week):

  • Getting to Know Me — Icebreakers to build community fast
  • Into the Bible — Scripture that supports or expands the sermon
  • Application — Drive home the sticky statement + action steps from Sunday

Tip for leaders: Start with 1–2 Getting to Know Me questions, spend the bulk of time on Application, and close with prayer. Keep it to 60–75 minutes. The goal is not to teach a new lesson — it's to help people process and live out last Sunday's message.

WEEK 1: “Jesus Never Said ‘Ask for Anything and You’ll Get It’” (Treating prayer like a vending machine or magic formula)
Sticky: Jesus never *meant* that if you just ask for anything in faith, you'll get it like a vending machine. He meant ask in line with His will, with pure motives, as children approaching a loving Father — not as a formula to get what we want.
Getting to Know Me
  • Have you ever been taught or believed that if you just have enough faith and ask God for something, He'll give it to you like a vending machine or magic formula? How has that affected your view of prayer or God?
  • When prayers haven't been answered the way you wanted (or at all), what thoughts or feelings came up for you about God or your faith?
Into the Bible
  • Read Matthew 7:7-11 in the full context of the Sermon on the Mount (especially around vv. 7-11 after "judge not" and the golden rule). What does "good things" mean here?
  • Read John 14:13-14 in context of Jesus preparing the disciples for mission and "greater works." What does "in my name" imply?
  • Read James 4:3 and 1 John 5:14. How do these verses correct the "ask for anything" formula?
  • Read Luke 22:42 (Jesus in Gethsemane) and 2 Corinthians 12:7-9 (Paul's thorn). How do these examples challenge the idea that we can always "claim" the outcome we want?
  • Read Daniel 3:16-18 (the three friends in the fire). What does their "but if not" show about true faith vs. demanding a specific result?
Application
  • The sticky from Sunday: "Jesus never *meant* that if you just ask for anything in faith, you'll get it like a vending machine. He meant ask in line with His will, with pure motives, as children approaching a loving Father — not as a formula to get what we want." Where have you seen (or believed) the "vending machine" version of prayer?
  • What is your current heart posture toward prayer? Is it transactional, anxious, relational, or something else?
  • Practical step this week: Take one big request you've been bringing to God and pray it with the Lord's Prayer framework — surrender "Your will be done" and thank Him for being a good Father.
Jesus never *meant* that if you just ask for anything in faith, you'll get it like a vending machine.
He meant ask in line with His will, with pure motives, as children approaching a loving Father — not as a formula to get what we want.
WEEK 2: “Jesus Never Said ‘Give and You’ll Get Rich’” (The prosperity gospel version of giving and blessing)
Sticky: Jesus never *meant* that giving is a formula for getting rich. He meant generosity flows from a transformed heart, not a transaction for blessing. You cannot serve both God and money.
Getting to Know Me
  • Have you ever been taught or believed that if you give generously (or "sow a seed"), God will make you rich or send a financial breakthrough? How has that affected your view of God or money?
  • When you see other Christians who seem very wealthy, what thoughts or feelings come up for you?
Into the Bible
  • Read Luke 6:38 in the full context of vv. 27-37 (loving enemies, being merciful). What does the "give" refer to?
  • Read 2 Corinthians 9:6-8 and Proverbs 11:24-25. What does Scripture actually teach about the heart posture of giving?
  • Read Mark 10:29-30. What does Jesus say comes "with persecutions"?
Application
  • The sticky from Sunday: "Jesus never *meant* that giving is a formula for getting rich. He meant generosity flows from a transformed heart, not a transaction for blessing. You cannot serve both God and money." Where have you seen (or believed) the "give to get rich" version?
  • What is your current heart posture toward money and giving? Is it transactional, fearful, generous, or something else?
  • Practical step this week: Give cheerfully without expecting a financial return. Examine one financial decision through the lens of "You cannot serve both God and money."
Jesus never *meant* that giving is a formula for getting rich.
He meant generosity flows from a transformed heart, not a transaction for blessing.
You cannot serve both God and money.
